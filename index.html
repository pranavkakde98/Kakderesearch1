The report

Can Global Investors Actually Capture India’s Economic Rise?

A dollar invested in India captures part of India’s rise — materially less than GDP growth implies. Return depends on corporate earnings, valuation, dilution, investability and currency translation. India is structurally attractive but valuation-sensitive, and must be sized against the markets competing for the same capital. An India-centred question, answered inside a genuine comparative emerging-markets framework.

  • MSCI India trailed MSCI EM over five and ten years in US dollars (8.03% vs 9.19% annualised, 10y net) — the fact most India research omits.
  • Over 20 years, India’s ~7.1% USD return was built on earnings, not the multiple (which added nothing) and net of a currency that subtracted ~3.8% a year.
  • Across 13 markets, GDP growth showed no reliable positive link to equity return. The marginal dollar bets on margins and the multiple, not on growth.

The method

Every market conclusion answers a second question: compared with what?

The markets that compete for a given allocation are named before any analysis begins, and the comparison set is chosen for the decision rather than inherited from a benchmark. India, China, Taiwan, Korea, Indonesia, Brazil, Mexico and the others are not interchangeable: they sit in different benchmarks, access regimes, liquidity conditions and valuation contexts. A country conclusion that cannot survive comparison with the markets competing for the same capital is not yet an investment conclusion.
