Kakde Research/Methodology

How the research is done

Six stages. The same six, whether the engagement is a one-question memo or a fifteen-day comparative study.

The six stages

  1. 01

    Define the question

    Most enquiries arrive as a topic rather than a question, and a topic cannot be answered. The scoping call turns it into something specific enough to be wrong about, by working backwards from the decision behind it. If that cannot be done, the engagement does not start.

  2. 02

    Establish the evidence

    Official data, primary sources, policy and regulatory documents, filings and market evidence, pulled and cleaned in-house. Where a series has been revised, redefined or interrupted, that is recorded rather than smoothed over, because those breaks are usually where the interesting answers sit.

  3. 03

    Challenge the consensus

    The prevailing view is written down explicitly, along with what it rests on. Often it rests on a single dataset, a definitional choice, or a comparison nobody has revisited in years. Knowing which one tells you how much weight the consensus can carry.

  4. 04

    Build the analysis

    Macroeconomic, financial and structural variables are connected into one argument rather than presented as parallel sections. The subject market — India most often, but any emerging market the question names — is set against the peers competing for the same capital, so the finding is comparative from the start and not benchmarked as an afterthought.

  5. 05

    Test the conclusion

    The finding is attacked before it is delivered. What alternative explanations produce the same result? Which parts survive if the weakest input is removed? Anything that only holds under one reading of the data is labelled as such in the report.

  6. 06

    Make it decision-relevant

    The report closes on what you should now understand differently and what follows from it, including the conditions that would change the view. A conclusion you cannot act on, or cannot defend when challenged, has not finished being researched.

Stage zero: choose the comparison

The comparison set is chosen, not inherited.

A good India analysis does not reach for China and the United States by reflex. A manufacturing question runs against Vietnam, Mexico, Indonesia and China. A domestic-demand question runs against Indonesia and Brazil. A commodity question runs against Brazil and South Africa. A market-access question runs against the whole classified set — and the classifications themselves differ by index provider, which is part of the analysis rather than a footnote.

The peer group is written into the scope before work starts, with the reasons. If the comparison set changes mid-engagement, that is recorded as a scope change — the same discipline the data gets.

Standards

  • Sources are recorded and disclosed

    Every figure in a report can be traced to its origin. If a number came from an estimate rather than a published series, the report says so and shows how the estimate was built.

  • The data appendix is reproducible

    The appendix is written so that someone on your side can rebuild the work. That is a deliberate constraint on the analysis, not a courtesy at the end of it.

  • Methodology is stated openly enough to be challenged

    Definitions, peer sets, date ranges and adjustments are stated in full. Research that cannot be argued with is not independent, it is marketing.

  • One revision round is included

    Delivery includes a call to challenge the findings and one written round of follow-up, so the report can absorb what you know that the analyst did not.

  • Where the evidence is thin, the report says so

    Some questions cannot be answered to the confidence a decision requires. Saying that plainly is more useful than a well-formatted guess, and it is what makes the confident findings worth reading.

  • What would change our view

    Important published conclusions carry the observable conditions that would weaken or invalidate them. A conclusion without a falsifier is an opinion wearing a chart.

Independence, in practice

The practice sells research and nothing else. There are no financial products behind the analysis, no execution business, no distribution agreements and no external capital with a view on the conclusions. Independence here is a matter of structure rather than a claim in a brochure.

The practical test is simple. Where India ranks badly against a comparator, the report says so first. That is what makes the favourable findings worth anything.
