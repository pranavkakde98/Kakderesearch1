Kakde Research / Methodology

From the question to the decision

A clear brief, traceable evidence and conclusions your team can question. The same discipline applies to a company assessment, an industry study or a market decision.

The six stages

From the question to the decision

  1. 01

    Define the decision

    Start with what your team needs to decide or understand. Establish the objective, constraints and deadline, then agree what the work must resolve.

  2. 02

    Set the scope

    Specify the company, industry, market or alternatives to examine. Agree the questions, outputs and boundaries before the analysis begins, whether for one assignment or a retained relationship.

  3. 03

    Build the evidence

    Work from company filings, official data, policy documents and other relevant sources. Record definitions, dates and limitations, and distinguish reported facts from estimates and interpretation.

  4. 04

    Develop the analysis

    Connect the evidence to the decision. Examine the economics, assumptions and alternatives that matter to the brief, showing how each finding follows from its inputs.

  5. 05

    Challenge the findings

    Test alternative explanations and the assumptions carrying the conclusion. Ask what changes if a weak input is removed or a key condition shifts, and make the remaining uncertainty explicit.

  6. 06

    Discuss the implications

    Explain what the findings mean for the original question, what remains unresolved and what would change the view. Discuss the work directly with your team and address the agreed follow-up.

Choose the relevant comparison

The scope follows the question

The comparison set is chosen, not inherited.

A company assessment may compare competitors, business models or customer segments. A market-entry brief may compare routes to customers or locations. An investment question may compare the opportunities available to the client. The comparison follows the decision.

A single country can be the whole assignment, including the US or a European market. For cross-market work, the relevant developed and emerging markets are included on the merits of the question. The scope records the comparison set and the reasons for choosing it.

Standards

Six, on every engagement

  • Sources are recorded and disclosed

    Every figure in a report can be traced to its origin. If a number came from an estimate rather than a published series, the report says so and shows how the estimate was built.

  • The data appendix is reproducible

    The appendix is written so that someone on your side can rebuild the work. That is a deliberate constraint on the analysis, not a courtesy at the end of it.

  • Methodology is stated openly enough to be challenged

    Definitions, peer sets, date ranges and adjustments are stated in full. Research that cannot be argued with is not independent, it is marketing.

  • One revision round is included

    Delivery includes a call to challenge the findings and one written round of follow-up, so the report can absorb what you know that the team did not.

  • Where the evidence is thin, the report says so

    Some questions cannot be answered to the confidence a decision requires. Saying that plainly is more useful than a well-formatted guess, and it is what makes the confident findings worth reading.

  • What would change our view

    Important published conclusions carry the observable conditions that would weaken or invalidate them. A conclusion without a falsifier is an opinion wearing a chart.

Independence, in practice

The practice sells research and consulting and nothing else. There are no financial products behind the analysis, no execution business, no distribution agreements and no external capital with a view on the conclusions. Independence here is a matter of structure rather than a claim in a brochure.

If the evidence weakens the investment case, commercial assumption or proposed course of action, the work should make that clear. The conclusion follows the evidence, including when it challenges the starting view.

Pranav leads the engagement and reviews the analysis. The analyst team develops and checks the supporting evidence. Direct access allows your team to question the reasoning with the people responsible for the work.
