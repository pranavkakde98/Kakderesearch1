The emerging markets data desk

India read two ways: against the global anchors it is always compared with, and against the emerging markets competing for the same capital. Every series is official, sourced, and dated — and every omission is documented.

What “live” means

Macro data is not tick data, and this desk does not pretend otherwise. Live means the desk shows the latest reported official observation for each series and is refreshed as the underlying source releases or revises. Annual figures do not flicker; they update when the world does. Each series carries its source ID, its unit, its observation years and its retrieval date — and a country whose latest report is too old to compare fairly shows n/a rather than a stale rank.

    Methodology, revisions and omissions

    Reading modes. Global anchors set India against China and the United States: the two systems every India allocation is implicitly measured against. EM peers set India against Indonesia, Brazil, Mexico, South Africa and Vietnam — a reading set, not a claim that these five exhaust the comparison. The right peer group depends on the question, and the written research chooses it question by question.

    Observations. All values are reported annual observations from World Bank Open Data (api.worldbank.org, API v2), retrieved 13 August 2026. Nothing is estimated, interpolated or extrapolated. Where countries report on different lags, each value carries its own year; the ranking table compares latest-available observations and withholds a rank (n/a) where a country’s latest report predates the indicator’s freshness threshold.

    Documented omissions. India’s 2025 listed-market-capitalisation observation in the source series is inconsistent with exchange-reported market capitalisation and is omitted rather than smoothed in; India shows 2024 for that row. Central government debt was retrieved but is not displayed: the World Bank central-government series ends in 2018 for India and is missing entirely for China and Vietnam, which makes a fair ranking impossible from this source. Vietnam’s remittances series ends in 2004 and is likewise not ranked.

    Derived series. The currency panel indexes each currency’s US-dollar value from annual-average official exchange rates (PA.NUS.FCRF), rebased to 100 at 2000. A falling line is depreciation against the dollar. This is the only transformation applied anywhere on the desk, and it is arithmetic, not estimation.

    Ranking shades by value, darkest highest. A rank is a reading order, not a verdict: high private credit is depth to one reader and leverage to another. The written research is where interpretation happens.
