Consulting · Project or ongoing advisory

You bring the decision. We research what it actually rests on.

Consulting here is research applied to a live decision: which market, whether to enter, whether the case holds, what a policy turn does to the plan. For investors, family offices, corporates and their advisers, across whichever markets the question names.

Available as a defined project or an ongoing advisory relationship. Scope, format and cadence are agreed for the engagement.

What consulting is here

Research applied to one decision.

An engagement is a piece of research with a decision at the end of it. Not a framework off the shelf, not a team billed by the week, not execution. One decision, researched to institutional standard against the alternatives it actually competes with.

The question sets the market. A mandate can run across developed markets, emerging markets or both; India is where the work goes deepest, not where it stops. The founder scopes every engagement and reviews every deliverable, and the people you speak to are the people doing the work.

What the work covers

Four broad groups · A hundred questions inside them

Four broad shapes, not a menu. The questions inside them are many, and some of the ways they arrive are listed further down. An engagement is scoped to the decision in front of you and may draw on more than one.

Opportunity & market comparison Entry, expansion & location Sector & competitive research Policy, scenarios & the board
01

Opportunity assessment and market comparison

Where should the capital, the plant or the plan go, and against what?

A screen of the universe against your objective, then a like-for-like comparison of the candidates that survive it, on one framework built for the decision: growth and demand, external position, credit, currency, valuation, access, governance, policy. The weights are stated. The verdict is argued, not averaged.

Lands as a shortlist you can defend to a committee, or a verdict between named markets, with the excluded candidates accounted for.

Not a portfolio recommendation, and not regulated advice. The decision stays with you.

02

Market entry, expansion and location

Enter, expand or wait, by which route, and where should the next plant or supplier sit?

How large the market is and how fast it is growing, from official statistics rather than vendor totals. How demand and channels are structured, who competes and how they win, what regulation and ownership rules shape the route in. For a footprint decision, which locations can take the volume at what cost and lead time, tested against the alternative of staying put. The China+1 question, across India, Vietnam, Indonesia, Mexico and Thailand, is the worked example.

Lands as an entry, expansion or relocation case a board can approve, defer or decline with the reasons on the table.

Not implementation, site selection, partner search or the legal and tax advice the route may need.

03

Sector and competitive research

How is the sector really structured, who earns in it, and where could a newcomer credibly position?

Concentration, competitors and their economics, growth drivers and their durability, pricing, margins, capital intensity, the regulatory frame and where it is moving, from filings, disclosures and official data. Where the sector spans markets, the picture is drawn on one frame so they can be read against each other.

Lands as a sector view you can act on: where the returns are made, who makes them, and what would have to be true for a newcomer to share in them.

Not built on non-public information; the work stays with filings, disclosures and official data. Not a recommendation on any security.

04

Policy, scenarios and board decision support

A policy has changed, or rates, currencies or trade may move. What does it do to your position, and how does the board test that?

The consequences of a specific measure, in any jurisdiction, traced through channels rather than headlines: rates, currency, credit, trade, costs, demand, ownership. A small number of internally consistent scenarios rather than a fan of everything, with the signposts that show which one is arriving. Where the decision is going to a committee, the work is built around the meeting and presented in the room by the people who did it.

Lands as what has actually changed for you, what has not, what to watch, and a decision stress-tested before it is made.

Not legal or tax advice on the measure. We inform the decision; we do not take it, vote on it or implement it.

The questions that arrive

Some of a hundred · One line each

Questions rarely arrive labelled by group. These are some of the ways they come through the door; if yours turns on evidence, it belongs here too.

  • A pre-IPO question

    What the market will pay for this business, on comparables and the evidence, before the roadshow says so.

  • A currency question

    What the currency has done to a foreign investor’s return, and what the regime implies for the next five years.

  • A deficit question

    Whether a government’s balance sheet can carry its plans, and at what rate.

  • A tariff question

    Who pays, who absorbs and who moves when a tariff lands on your supply chain.

  • A rates question

    What a change in the cost of capital does to a valuation, a project or a portfolio.

  • A market-selection question

    Which of five markets deserves the next allocation, measured like for like.

  • An entry-route question

    Organic, partnership, acquisition or greenfield, for this market and this appetite.

  • A location question

    Where the next plant, supplier or hub should sit, for this sector, at what cost and lead time.

  • A sector question

    Who competes, who earns, and what lets a newcomer in.

  • A policy question

    What a budget, a central-bank turn or a new ownership rule does to your position.

  • A scenario question

    If rates, currencies or demand move, what happens to the plan, and what shows which way it is going.

  • A thesis question

    What your existing view fails to account for, argued against you before the market does it.

  • A committee question

    What evidence should reach the board, in what form, and who defends it in the room.

  • A peer-set question

    Compared with what? The alternatives, named before the analysis is written.

  • A data question

    Whether the series your model rests on says what you think it says, and where it breaks.

  • And yours.

    If it turns on evidence and a decision hangs on it, bring it. The scoping call decides whether it can be answered well.

How a mandate is framed

Six lines · Agreed in writing

It starts with a scoping call. If the question cannot be answered well, you hear that on the call, before anything is spent. What follows is written down, the same six lines whether the work is a project or the next question inside an ongoing relationship.

The decision
What is being decided, by whom and by when.
The alternatives
What it is really a choice between, named before any analysis is written.
The comparators
The markets, peers or jurisdictions the answer runs against.
The evidence
Official data, filings and primary documents, assembled and dated in-house. Gaps stated, not filled.
The judgement
Labelled as judgement, with the conditions that would change it written beside it.
The output
Report, memo, deck, model, briefing or live presentation. Format, timing and fee agreed up front; one round of revision included.

Consulting and research fees are the only revenue. No products, no execution, no external capital. A conclusion is allowed to be inconvenient.

How the work arrives

Agreed for the engagement

  1. 01Research report: the full argument, sources dated, data shown
  2. 02Executive memo: the answer, written for the committee pack
  3. 03Committee or board presentation: built to be presented, or forwarded
  4. 04Economic or financial model: with assumptions your side can change
  5. 05Data appendix or dashboard: every series sourced, dated, rebuildable
  6. 06In the room: live presentation, workshop, or a call before the meeting and after it

The format follows the decision. In an ongoing relationship the formats recur on the agreed rhythm, with rapid responses between them.
