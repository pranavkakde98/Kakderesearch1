The premise

Most India research is written a long way from India.

India is the market Kakde Research covers most deeply and most continuously. The India Desk combines that continuous domestic research with comparison against the emerging markets competing for the same portfolio capital — a local reading with the discipline of an institutional desk, rather than coverage assembled from the same secondary sources the client already reads.

The problem

The exposure justifies the questions. It rarely justifies the headcount.

Covering India properly is not one job. It is a dedicated India economist, a macro strategist, a policy analyst, a financial-markets analyst, an EM comparative analyst, a data analyst and a research writer — a desk. For an organisation whose India exposure is meaningful but not dominant, that desk never clears the bar.

The questions do not wait for the hire. They arrive on their own schedule:

  • Before every investment-committee meeting with India on the agenda.

  • After an RBI decision, a Budget, an election, a policy reversal.

  • After a sharp move in the rupee, or a change in foreign-ownership rules.

  • When index composition shifts, and the India weight moves without you trading.

  • When India trades at a growing premium and someone has to say whether it is justified.

  • When the China allocation changes and the EM ex-China question lands on India.

  • When a China+1 thesis needs testing against Vietnam, Mexico and Indonesia.

  • When the model’s India assumptions have not been re-examined since they were set.

The India Desk fills that gap: standing research capacity, already up to speed on your context, without the fixed cost of building the team.

What the desk delivers

Seven modules, scoped to fit

Not every retainer carries every module. The engagement is designed around the questions your organisation actually faces, and the scope is written down before it starts.

01 · Monthly research note

A bespoke research paper each month, built around an India question you nominate — or an India-relative-to-EM question the desk believes you should be asking. Written to committee standard, with sources and the data appendix shown.

02 · Rapid written Q&A

Short-turnaround analytical responses between the major notes, in writing, within 48 hours. The questions that cannot wait for a report cycle mostly should not have to.

03 · Monthly research discussion

A structured conversation around the month’s note, the EM comparison, and whatever the month raised — with the analyst who did the work, not an account manager relaying it.

04 · Comparative EM monitoring

India’s position tracked against the peers relevant to your allocation — Indonesia, Vietnam, Brazil, Mexico, South Africa, or the set your committee actually debates — rather than a fixed basket.

05 · Maintained data

Continuously maintained datasets, comparisons and dashboards behind the written work, refreshed as the sources release, with each revision noted.

06 · Priority research capacity

A desk that already knows your portfolio and your priors. New questions start from context, not from a cold scoping exercise every time.

07 · Deep-dive research

Where a question outgrows the monthly note, it graduates into a dedicated paper — scoped and quoted separately, with the retainer’s context already priced in.

What it is not

The retainer is analyst capacity, not a product feed. It does not manage assets, execute trades, sell financial products or provide personalised regulated investment advice.

The desk answers compared with what?

The emerging-markets lens

What happened in India is available from the wires, the banks and the same sources everyone reads. The India Desk exists for the harder half: whether it makes India more or less attractive relative to the alternatives. The kind of question it is built for:

  • Does the latest RBI move improve India’s relative attractiveness against Indonesia?

  • Is India’s inflation trajectory actually better than the EM peer set’s?

  • Does rupee risk offset India’s stronger equity-growth profile?

  • Is India’s valuation premium justified relative to Brazil?

  • If China risk recedes, does India’s relative allocation case weaken?

  • Does Vietnam offer better manufacturing exposure for this China+1 thesis?

  • Is India truly the natural beneficiary of EM ex-China allocations?

  • Where does India rank on current-account resilience, real rates, credit depth?

The comparison runs both ways. Where India wins it, the note says so with the evidence attached. Where it does not — where Vietnam takes the manufacturing metric or Brazil is cheaper for the growth — the note says that first.

Bespoke by design

No off-the-shelf subscription tiers. Each India Desk engagement is a bespoke monthly retainer designed around the organisation’s actual India and emerging-markets research requirements — the modules above are the vocabulary, not the menu.

Like every engagement here: the coverage, the deliverables and the monthly fee are agreed in writing before the desk opens. A retainer that grows is re-scoped, not silently re-priced.

The researcher you speak to is the researcher who does the work. No account-manager handoff, no junior team separated from your question — which caps how many desks run at once, deliberately.

Independence

No brokerage, no execution, no product distribution, no asset-management incentive. Nothing about the desk earns more when the conclusion goes one way rather than the other.
