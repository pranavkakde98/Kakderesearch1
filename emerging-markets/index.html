Kakde Research / Emerging Markets

No emerging market exists in a portfolio vacuum.

Kakde Research studies emerging economies against the alternatives competing for the same capital. India is where our coverage runs deepest; the comparison set around it is chosen for the question — China, Indonesia, Vietnam, Brazil, Mexico, Taiwan, Korea, South Africa, selected Gulf markets, or the broad EM index — not fixed in advance.

The questions an EM allocation actually turns on

A growth story is not an investment. Between a fast-growing economy and a return in a foreign investor’s currency sit a series of filters: whether growth reaches corporate earnings, whether earnings reach the listed market, whether a foreigner may own that market, at what valuation, in which currency, and under what global regime. We work each filter explicitly, and we work it comparatively.

How we cover emerging markets

Eight dimensions, one comparison

Each dimension is analysed relative to the markets competing for the same allocation, not in isolation. India is the deepest reading; the comparators are chosen per question. Continuous specialist India coverage sits on the India Desk.

01

Country Allocation

Which market deserves the marginal dollar, and on what evidence?

What we study

Relative growth, valuation, currency and investability across the candidate set, read as one allocation decision rather than a stack of single-country notes.

Example question

For a fixed EM equity budget, does the next unit belong in India, Indonesia or a return to a China position — and what would have to be true for the ranking to flip?

02

Cross-Market Valuation

Which valuation premiums the fundamentals actually support.

What we study

Price against earnings, ROE, currency and growth, on a common basis. A premium backed by durable return on equity is not the same asset as a premium backed by a story, even where the multiple is identical.

Example question

How much of India’s valuation premium survives once it is adjusted for ROE and earnings durability against the EM set?

03

Capital Flows & FX

Who sets the price at the margin, and what a dollar turn changes.

What we study

Foreign portfolio flows, domestic institutional flows, reserves, the dollar cycle, rates and currencies — and the balance between foreign and local capital that decides how much a currency move actually costs a foreign holder.

Example question

What does a sustained weaker dollar change across EM currencies and valuations, and which markets have the domestic bid to absorb a foreign exit?

04

Trade & Manufacturing

China+1, and the harder question of who captures the value.

What we study

Supply chains, export share, domestic value added and the split of the gains between the host economy, the multinational and the local listed shareholder. A rising export line is not the same as a rising domestic return.

Example question

India, Vietnam or Mexico for the China+1 electronics build-out — and in each, how much of the value reaches a company a foreigner can own?

05

Domestic Demand

Consumption, credit and financialisation as an investable exposure.

What we study

Household consumption, the credit cycle, the financialisation of domestic savings, and how much of a listed market’s earnings actually depend on the domestic economy rather than exports or global commodity prices.

Example question

India or Indonesia for domestic-demand exposure — and how much of each equity market’s earnings is genuinely local?

06

Market Structure & Investability

The gap between the economy and the slice a foreigner can own.

What we study

Free float, ownership limits, promoter and state holdings, governance, index construction and foreign-access routes — the mechanics that decide whether economic weight ever becomes investable weight.

Example question

Across the EM set, where is the gap between GDP weight and ownable free float widest — and where is it closing?

07

Political Economy

Policy, institutions and state capacity, where they move the numbers.

What we study

The policy framework, institutional quality and state capacity of a market — covered where it changes an economic or market outcome an allocator holds, not as standalone political commentary.

Example question

Which of two comparable growth stories rests on institutions durable enough to survive a change of government?

08

Global Transmission

The Fed, the dollar, oil and trade, through their effect on EM.

What we study

US rates, the dollar, oil and commodities, and trade shocks — analysed through the channel by which they reach emerging-market earnings, currencies and valuations, not for their own sake.

Example question

What does $100 oil do to the external balances and USD equity returns of India versus Indonesia versus Brazil?

Example research questions

Illustrative · not a list of past mandates

  • India or Indonesia for domestic-demand exposure — and how much of each actually reaches a foreign shareholder?

  • What does sustained US rate pressure mean for EM currencies and equity valuations?
