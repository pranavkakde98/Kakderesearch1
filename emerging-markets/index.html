The premise

Global capital allocates comparatively. So should the research behind it.

No emerging market can be understood only through its own domestic narrative. The dollar weighing India is also weighing Indonesia and Mexico, the broad index, and staying home. The plant that might go to Vietnam might go to Mexico. A country conclusion that cannot survive contact with the alternatives is not yet a conclusion. It is a description.

So the desk starts every question with a second one: compared with what? The peer set is chosen for the decision, not inherited from a benchmark. A manufacturing question runs against Vietnam, Mexico, Indonesia and China. A domestic-demand question runs against Indonesia and Brazil. A market-access question runs across the whole classified EM set. Getting that choice right is the first task of any engagement, and most of the analytical value sits in it.

Global forces such as the dollar, US rates, oil, trade policy and China’s cycle do not hit emerging markets equally. They transmit through different current accounts, reserve positions, debt structures and export mixes into different allocation and business decisions. Reading that transmission across markets, in one frame, is what the desk is for.

The problem

The exposure justifies the questions. It rarely justifies the headcount.

Covering several emerging markets properly is not one job. It is an economist, a strategist, a policy analyst, a markets analyst and a data analyst for each, and someone whose job is the comparison between them. For an organisation whose exposure is meaningful but not dominant, that team never clears the bar. The questions still arrive:

  • Before every committee with an emerging-market line on the agenda.

  • After a US rates move, a dollar turn or an oil shock: which of our markets does it actually hurt?

  • When one market re-rates and the question becomes whether to fund it from another.

  • When the China allocation changes and EM ex-China has to be reasoned about, not assumed.

  • When a China+1 thesis needs testing against Vietnam, Mexico, Indonesia and India on evidence.

  • When an election, a policy reversal or a currency regime change alters the case for one market and not its neighbours.

  • When a board asks which markets deserve deeper attention, and why not the others.

  • When the model’s country assumptions have not been re-examined since they were set.

The Emerging Markets Desk fills that gap: standing comparative research capacity, already up to speed on your markets and your priors, without the fixed cost of building the team. A question that spans developed and emerging markets is scoped under Consulting, with the desk supplying the emerging-market depth, on the same terms.

What the desk delivers

The vocabulary, not the menu

Not every desk carries every element. The engagement is designed around the questions your organisation actually faces and the markets it holds, and the scope is written down before it opens.

01 · Recurring comparative notes

Research on the cross-market questions your universe raises: the decision the month actually produced, answered comparatively, on a cadence agreed with you. Written to committee standard, with sources and the data shown.

02 · Country and sector dashboards

Maintained scorecards across the agreed markets covering growth, prices, external accounts, credit, currency, valuation and access, refreshed as the sources release, every figure sourced and dated.

03 · Event-driven briefs

When rates, the dollar, oil, trade policy, an election or a regulatory turn changes a decision across the set: what moved, which markets it reaches, and what it changes for you.

04 · Rapid research response

Short-turnaround analysis for questions that cannot wait for the regular cycle. Depending on the issue, the response may take the form of a concise memo, a data note, a briefing deck, a research call or a video discussion.

05 · Scenario analysis

Structured scenarios around the variables that matter for your markets, from rates, currencies and geopolitics to trade, regulation and demand, traced through to positioning or strategy, with the assumptions stated plainly enough to be argued with.

06 · Research calls and committee briefings

Live discussion with the people who did the work; presentations and briefing decks for investment committees, strategy teams and boards, built to be forwarded rather than summarised.

07 · Deep-dive research

Where a question outgrows the note it graduates into a dedicated study, whether a market comparison, an entry assessment or a supply-chain map, scoped inside the desk’s context rather than from zero.

What it is not

The desk is analyst capacity, not a product feed. It does not manage assets, execute trades, sell financial products or provide personalised regulated investment advice.

How the desk runs

Four steps, then a standing capability

  1. 01

    Define the universe

    The markets you hold, the markets you are weighing, and the ones that only matter as comparators. Broad or focused; it can change by agreement as your exposure does.

  2. 02

    Agree the questions and the cadence

    What recurs, what is event-driven, who on your side receives what and in which format. Written down before the desk opens.

  3. 03

    The desk runs

    Notes, dashboards, briefs and calls arrive on the agreed rhythm; rapid responses and deep dives inside it. The team already holds your context, so no question starts cold.

  4. 04

    Re-scope as the mandate moves

    A market enters or leaves the set, a theme becomes standing, a cadence changes. The engagement is re-scoped in writing, not silently re-priced.

    The engagement, stated plainly

    No tiers, no cards, no packages

    Bespoke by design

    No off-the-shelf subscription. Each Emerging Markets Desk engagement, whether a specific assignment or a retained relationship, is designed around the organisation’s actual markets, questions and decision calendar. The modules above are the vocabulary, not the menu.

    Scoped in writing

    The covered universe, the deliverables, the formats and the fee are agreed in writing before work starts. A relationship that grows is re-scoped, not silently re-priced; a question outside the agreed scope is agreed as a separate assignment.

    Founder-led, directly accessible

    Kakde Research is founder-led. Pranav Kakde scopes every desk, reviews the work and stays on the client relationship; a team of six analysts assembles, checks and maintains the evidence.     Independence

    No brokerage, no execution, no product distribution, no asset-management incentive. Nothing about the desk earns more when the conclusion favours one market over another.
